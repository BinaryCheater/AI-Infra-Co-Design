# Simulator gaps

Designs the mechanistic model could not match **without changing a measured parameter**, logged as
missing mechanisms per `notes/mechanistic_simulator_design.md` §4. Measured parameters come from
`calibration/spark-6951/contract.yaml` and are never fitted; structural choices are in
`agentic/sim/config/spark-6951-gb10.yaml` with the benchmark that justified each.

| # | Design / benchmark | Simulated vs measured | What is missing | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4096³ GEMM, 128 × 256 × 16 tiles, S = 2, P = 1, β = 1 (spark-001, iteration 1): 9395.7 µs measured | G2 (L2 + DRAM only): 5057 µs (−46 %), critical path `barrier`/compute in every phase | **M3, shared-memory reads during compute.** Each k-step of the generated kernel issues 8 + 16 `ld.shared` per thread (393 KB per phase per block); at the measured 154 GB/s per SM that is 2.5 µs per phase against 1.8 µs of FMA. G2 has no shared-memory mechanism, so the phase is FMA-bound in the model and smem-bound on the card. | **After G3 (M3 + M4): 7414 µs (−21 %)**, critical path `smem` in 123 of 128 phases. The FMA pipe (1.8 µs/phase) and the `ld.shared` pipe (2.56 µs/phase at the measured 154 GB/s) overlap only partly on the card (3.3 µs/phase measured). What is missing is instruction issue: both pipes are fed by the same warp schedulers and each k-step's loads precede its FMAs, which §1 puts out of scope. Also open: whether B6's 154 GB/s is the LDS pipe's rate or the benchmark's dependent-chain limit. **B21 answers it:** independent `ld.shared` at 32/64/128-bit widths runs at 82 / 77 / 38 G instructions/s per SM = 0.33 / 0.62 / 0.62 TB/s per SM, so the pipe is 4x faster than B6's chain and the smem pipe is not the 4096³ bottleneck by itself; B21's inner-loop probe runs the T1 loop at 46 % of the FFMA bound with the tile already in smem, i.e. the loss is LDS + FFMA *issue* from the same schedulers (B17: the SM issues at most 4 warp-instructions per cycle), which is the mechanism §1 put out of scope. Not fitted. |
| 2 | B8 cp.async latency vs tile size | depth 370 (from the 4 → 48 KB slope) reproduces the endpoints within 5 %; 8–32 KB are +9 % to +20 % slow | The per-SM copy rate is not constant in tile size (≈ 62 GB/s at 4 → 8 KB, 37 GB/s at 32 → 48 KB): a single outstanding-chunk window gives a straight line. Likely the issue side (cp.async instruction issue and address arithmetic per chunk, 1–12 instructions per thread) adding to the flow; needs a B8 variant that separates issue from completion. | logged; B8 test holds the endpoints at 5 % and the middle at 25 % |
| 3 | 4096³ waves | the measured run is 512 blocks on 48 SMs (10.7 waves); the simulator's breadth-first scheduler starts the last partial wave with 32 idle SMs | not a mismatch in itself, but the scheduler's dispatch order is unverified (B9 fixes capacity, not order) | **B16 measured it:** the first wave is assigned as SM pairs round-robin over six groups of eight (0,1,8,9,16,17,…), identical on every launch; later waves follow completion order (identical orderings 10 % of launches), with a 192 ns refill from exit to the next entry on the same SM. The breadth-first choice is right for the first wave; the simulator does not model the pair/group order, which matters only for L2-slice or GPC-local effects B13 did not find. |
| 4 | B13 L2 structure vs G2's phenomenological L2 | G2 models the L2 as LRU over 1 KB regions with capacity `l2_bytes_effective` = 16 MiB (B4's streaming knee) | B13: a single-warp chase keeps 91 % hits at 24 MiB and collapses at 32 MiB in both sequential and random order (LRU-like, declared capacity reachable), 64 B fill granularity, no set conflict up to 64 lines at any power-of-two stride, and a 5 ns latency spread over address bits (no near/far slices). So the 16 MiB knee is a bandwidth effect of many streams, not a capacity: the model's capacity should be 24 MiB with a bandwidth that falls between 16 and 24 MiB. | logged; G2's capacity choice is now marked phenomenological against B13's measured 24 MiB chase capacity (`l2_chase_capacity_bytes`); not changed in this round |
| 5 | B26 TMA visibility | the verifier's M4 reading treats the destination as undefined until the mbarrier phase completes | B26.4: bytes of a TMA tile are readable before `mbarrier.test_wait` reports completion (60594 early bytes over 2000 trials of 16 KB), while `cp.async` chunks land strictly in issue order and never torn. The untimed reading stays conservative (undefined until completion); a timed model that charged a read-before-wait as a certain failure would be wrong. | logged; no model change |
| 6 | B17 issue width | M7 charges compute at the FMA rate with no issue model | B17: one SM issues at most 4 warp-instructions per cycle (4 schedulers x 1), FMA/IMAD latency 4 cycles, LDS 33, L1 hit 44; B21: the generated inner loops reach 45 % (T5) and 46 % (T1) of the FFMA bound with the tile in smem. An issue-limited compute model (LDS + FFMA + address arithmetic per warp-cycle budget) would close most of gap 1. | hypothesis for G4 |
