# Simulator gaps

Designs the mechanistic model could not match **without changing a measured parameter**, logged as
missing mechanisms per `notes/mechanistic_simulator_design.md` §4. Measured parameters come from
`calibration/spark-6951/contract.yaml` and are never fitted; structural choices are in
`agentic/sim/config/spark-6951-gb10.yaml` with the benchmark that justified each.

| 1 | 4096³ GEMM, 128 × 256 × 16 tiles, S = 2, P = 1, β = 1 (spark-001, iteration 1): 9395.7 µs measured | G2 (L2 + DRAM only): 5057 µs (−46 %), critical path `barrier`/compute in every phase | **M3, shared-memory reads during compute.** Each k-step of the generated kernel issues 8 + 16 `ld.shared` per thread (393 KB per phase per block); at the measured 154 GB/s per SM that is 2.5 µs per phase against 1.8 µs of FMA. G2 has no shared-memory mechanism, so the phase is FMA-bound in the model and smem-bound on the card. | **After G3 (M3 + M4): 7414 µs (−21 %)**, critical path `smem` in 123 of 128 phases. The FMA pipe (1.8 µs/phase) and the `ld.shared` pipe (2.56 µs/phase at the measured 154 GB/s) overlap only partly on the card (3.3 µs/phase measured). What is missing is instruction issue: both pipes are fed by the same warp schedulers and each k-step's loads precede its FMAs, which §1 puts out of scope. Also open: whether B6's 154 GB/s is the LDS pipe's rate or the benchmark's dependent-chain limit. **B21 answers it:** independent `ld.shared` at 32/64/128-bit widths runs at 82 / 77 / 38 G instructions/s per SM = 0.33 / 0.62 / 0.62 TB/s per SM, so the pipe is 4x faster than B6's chain and the smem pipe is not the 4096³ bottleneck by itself; B21's inner-loop probe runs the T1 loop at 46 % of the FFMA bound with the tile already in smem, i.e. the loss is LDS + FFMA *issue* from the same schedulers (B17: the SM issues at most 4 warp-instructions per cycle), which is the mechanism §1 put out of scope.
